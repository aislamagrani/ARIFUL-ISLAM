01 / About

A technical lens on industrial credit

I work in the Industrial Credit Division of a state-owned commercial bank in Bangladesh, where I appraise industrial project loan proposals from start to finish. That covers technical, financial and marketing viability, credit memo preparation and credit valuation.

My financial feasibility work covers net present value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR), payback period, profitability index, benefit-cost ratio (BCR), cash flow projections, return on equity, return on investment and loan amortization. I have applied these to spinning, dyeing and printing, weaving and knitting, and garments projects.

I work within Bangladesh Bank guidelines, loan classification standards and CIB data requirements. My textile engineering training in wet processing lets me test a project’s machinery, process flow, capacity and raw material assumptions before the numbers are modelled.

The appraiser’s lens

One project, two readings. Choose a textile sub-sector to see what the engineer and the banker each look for before a loan is recommended.

The engineer checks

  • Machine configuration, age and condition
  • Yarn count mix and product range
  • Realistic capacity utilisation and waste
  • Power supply and humidity control

The banker checks

  • Cotton price swings and import dependence
  • Working capital cycle for fibre stock
  • Local demand from knitters and weavers
  • Effect of power cost on margin

The engineer checks

  • Loom and knitting machine type, speed and efficiency
  • Fabric mix and GSM range
  • Skill level of operators and supervisors
  • Access to yarn and dyeing capacity

The banker checks

  • Order pipeline and buyer concentration
  • Backward linkage with spinning
  • Price pressure from competing units
  • Cash flow against the order cycle

The engineer checks

  • Process flow and machine capacity
  • Water, steam and chemical consumption
  • Effluent treatment plant design and compliance
  • Shade matching and testing laboratory

The banker checks

  • Cost of environmental compliance
  • Gas, steam and chemical price exposure
  • Dependence on garment export orders
  • Imported chemical lead times

The engineer checks

  • Line layout, machine count and skill mix
  • Daily production capacity versus orders
  • Compliance and buyer audit readiness
  • Quality control and rework rate

The banker checks

  • Buyer orders, lead times and concentration
  • Back-to-back L/C exposure
  • Margin pressure from wages and prices
  • Export documentation and realization cycle
